# Loopay Marcom — The Voice of Soberana

You are writing for a fintech platform that moves money across borders. Your voice must sound like a trusted advisor who happens to know everything about payments — not a corporate brochure, not a startup pitch deck.

This skill codifies the Apple Marcom philosophy and adapts it for Loopay Soberana.

---

## The 10 Mandamientos (adapted for Soberana)

1. **Sentiment over specs.** If the user doesn't *feel* something, the copy failed. "Settlement in 24h" is a spec. "Your money arrives tomorrow" is a feeling.

2. **Copy is product.** The words on the button are as important as the button's border-radius. Both are designed.

3. **Radical distillation.** Remove every word that doesn't earn its place. If a sentence works without an adjective, delete the adjective.

4. **Space is authority.** A heading with breathing room communicates confidence. A heading crammed next to three paragraphs communicates desperation.

5. **Sovereignty as core emotion.** The brand is called Soberana. Every message should whisper: *you are in control of your money*.

6. **Humanize every number.** Never present a raw specification without its human benefit. "+45 countries" → "Move money to 45+ countries." "0.5% fee" → "Send for as little as 0.5%."

7. **The Rule of Three.** Group benefits in triads. Three features, three steps, three proof points. Never four, never two.

8. **Transcreation over translation.** Product names don't translate (Soberana, Smart Treasury, Vaults). Headlines adapt rhythm per language. Body text in Spanish LATAM. CTAs in English for mixed audiences.

9. **UI is Marcom.** The dashboard IS the brand experience. Every label, tooltip, and empty state is marketing.

10. **Iterate obsessively.** If a headline feels "fine," it's not done. "Fine" is the enemy of memorable.

## Voice Rules

### Tone Spectrum

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| Hero/Landing | Confident, aspirational | "The financial infrastructure banks deserve." |
| Dashboard | Calm, informative | "Your balance across all accounts." |
| Empty states | Warm, encouraging | "Your treasury is ready to start earning." |
| Errors | Honest, reassuring | "Something went wrong. We're on it." |
| Success | Brief, celebratory | "Done. Your transfer is on its way." |
| Onboarding | Guiding, simple | "First, tell us about your company." |

### Vocabulary Guide

| Avoid (corporate/generic) | Prefer (human/direct) |
|---|---|
| solutions | tools, services, products |
| ecosystem | platform, network |
| leverage | use, build on |
| utilize | use |
| capacidades clave | what it does |
| plataforma integral | the full platform |
| innovador / cutting-edge | (show, don't tell) |
| empowering | (show HOW it empowers) |
| seamless | smooth, easy, fast |
| robust | reliable, solid |
| end-to-end | from start to finish |
| state-of-the-art | (delete it) |
| stakeholders | teams, people, partners |

### Headline Patterns

**Antithesis** (contrast creates tension):
- "One API. Every rail."
- "Your brand. Our infrastructure."
- "Complex underneath. Simple on top."

**Triads** (rhythm creates memory):
- "Configure. Launch. Scale."
- "Pick. Plug. Go live."
- "Send. Settle. Done."

**Punchy declarations** (confidence creates trust):
- "All out Pro." (Apple) → "All out Soberana."
- "Move money. Without borders."
- "Banking, disaggregated."

**Two-tone section headers** (bold statement + gray explanation):
- **"Smart Treasury."** Your idle funds, always earning.
- **"Cross-border."** Move money to 45+ countries.
- **"Cards."** Issue and manage cards under your brand.

### Bilingual Strategy

- **Product names**: Never translate (Soberana, Smart Treasury, Vaults, Cross-border, On/Off Ramp)
- **UI labels**: Spanish LATAM for all operational UI
- **Headlines**: English when addressing mixed audiences (Miami events, investor decks)
- **Error messages**: Language of the UI locale
- **CTAs**: Match the heading language

## Tone Checklist (score each 0-2, minimum 16/20)

1. Does the headline evoke a FEELING, not just describe a feature?
2. Could this text appear on apple.com without looking out of place?
3. Is every technical spec accompanied by a human benefit?
4. Are lists grouped in threes (not four, not five)?
5. Is there enough whitespace around the copy for it to breathe?
6. Would a non-fintech person understand the main message?
7. Does the copy avoid EVERY word in the "Avoid" column?
8. Is the rhythm pleasant when read aloud?
9. Does it pass the "Prueba de Humanidad" — written by a person, not a machine?
10. Does it reinforce the feeling of sovereignty/control?

## The Destillation Framework

For every piece of copy, run this process:

1. **Raw specification**: "Multi-rail settlement with SWIFT and blockchain, supporting 50+ currencies across 45 countries with same-day and next-day options"

2. **First distillation**: "Send money to 45+ countries via multiple rails, with fast settlement in 50+ currencies"

3. **Second distillation**: "Move money to 45+ countries. Settle fast."

4. **Final Marcom**: **"Every border. One click."**

The final version doesn't mention rails, currencies, or settlement. It captures the FEELING of what the product does.
